Dictation that cleans your ums, not your meaning.

Free, open-source dictation for Mac. Hold Right ⌘, speak, let go. Clean text lands at your cursor in any app. Your voice and text never leave your Mac.

Dictation

  • Clean Verbatim coming soon

    Deletes hesitations, stutters and whatever you take back with “scratch that.” It only ever deletes, so it has no way to write a word you didn’t say. When a word could be real, it stays: “um” goes only with a comma of its own, acronyms and doubles like “very very” stay, and code and paths are never touched.

    I I think that the tests are green, uh, mostly.

    Um 5 Uhr treffen wir uns im Büro. German “um” is a word, so it stays.

    For example, “I I think that the tests are green, uh, mostly” becomes “I think that the tests are green, mostly”, while the German “Um 5 Uhr treffen wir uns im Büro” stays as it is, because there “um” is a word.

  • As spoken coming soon

    Open any take in History and see it the way you said it, with every deletion struck through. Copy the raw words whenever you need them.

  • “Send it.” coming soon

    End a take with “Send it.” in a terminal, and OpenRamble presses Return for you. It works only in terminals and only as its own last sentence, so a “send” in the middle of your text stays text.

  • Shift for English coming soon

    Hold Shift with the dictation key and speak another language. English comes out, translated on your Mac by Apple’s on-device translation. Needs macOS 26.4 or later and the language installed.

  • Any app, clipboard intact

    Text goes in at the cursor, wherever it is. Your clipboard comes back byte for byte, and dictated text is marked so it skips Universal Clipboard and clipboard managers.

Recording

  • Meeting recorder

    Records your microphone and the other side of the call, and transcribes both on your Mac while you record, labelled You and Others. Recordings stay until you delete them.

    Ask people before you record them. In many places it is the law.

  • Screen recording

    Record a display with your voice, Mac audio and a camera bubble you can move while you talk. You get an MP4 next to the transcript, on your Mac. No upload, no share links.

  • Command line

    Transcribe files from the terminal with the model the app already installed. TXT, JSON, SRT or VTT. A skill for Claude Code and Codex is in the repository.

    openramble interview.m4a --format srt > interview.srt

Verify it yourself

Your voice and text never leave your Mac. You don’t have to take our word for it.

  1. Read the gate

    Every change runs check-network-surface.sh in CI. It fails the build if shipping code calls a network API outside two declared places: model downloads and updates.

  2. Run it offline

    In a clone of the repository, with the model installed. The first runs recognition with the network blocked and checks the text. The second traces every network call and fails if recognition makes one.

    ./scripts/test-zero-network.sh
./scripts/test-zero-network-trace.sh

  3. Watch the wire

    Open LuLu or Little Snitch and dictate. Recognition makes no connections. These three are the only ones the app ever makes:

    Every connection the app makes
    ConnectionGoes toWhen
    Model downloadHugging Face, GitHub as a fallbackOnce, when you click Download
    Update checkGitHub PagesOn a schedule. Turn it off in Settings → Updates
    Update downloadGitHub ReleasesOnly after you click Install

    The update check sends your IP address and app version, nothing about your Mac.

  4. Check this page

    No analytics, no trackers, nothing loaded from anyone else. Open your browser’s network panel and reload.

Measured in public, losses included

200 public recordings through each app on one MacBook Pro M4 Pro. The data, the scripts and every raw output are published.

  • Same words as Handy. Both run the same Parakeet model file and produced the same words on all 200 recordings.
  • Steadier on a busy Mac. With every core kept busy, a short file took a median 0.51 s in OpenRamble and 1.13 s in Handy. Without the load, the two are close.
  • Whisper made fewer errors. whisper.cpp with Large v3 Turbo had a lower word error rate in both languages on this sample.

Limits

  • 25 European languages, including English, Russian, German, French, Spanish and Ukrainian. Chinese, Japanese and Korean are not supported.
  • Mac first. Built for Apple Silicon. Intel runs recognition on the processor and is experimental.
  • Long mixed-language recordings are weak. An hour alternating Russian and English came out at 39% word error rate. We are working on it.
